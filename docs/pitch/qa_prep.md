# Jury Q&A prep

Short, honest answers. Each figure is copied from the file named next to it. If a number changes, update this file from that file; do not type new ones.

**1. How accurate is the damage detection?**
- We do not have an accuracy number yet. Phase 2, the evaluation on a local test set of damaged cars, is pending.
- Our only test car was undamaged, so every detection on it was a false positive. The full pipeline reported 11 findings on it (`outputs/e2e/run1/result.json`; `docs/research/smoke_test_results.md`, Observations 3).
- We show this openly rather than claim a metric.

**2. What about night and rain?**
- Not measured yet. CLAUDE.md section 8 requires day / night / wet slices, and they are part of Phase 2.
- The quality filter (sharpness and brightness) uses uncalibrated default thresholds (`outputs/smoke/R1-local-gpu/summary.md`).

**3. What about false positives on reflections?**
- They are the main failure we have seen.
- "Glass shatter" fires on windshields that reflect sky and trees. "Scratch" and "dent" fire on door decals (Observations 3).
- Fixes:
  - calibrate thresholds per class on Phase 2 data;
  - require the same damage in several frames (`dedupe.merge`);
  - the advisor confirms each finding before the PDF.
- An optional false-positive filter exists. Its card reports clean-car false alarms falling from 75% to 4% (`docs/research/model_candidates.md`). This is reported by its author; we have not tested it.

**4. Licences: can Royal Motors use this commercially?**
- **Not as is.**
- The repo is AGPL-3.0, because every Ultralytics checkpoint is AGPL. The deployed app links to its source (ADR 003).
- The damage weights were trained on CarDD, which is for non-commercial research and education. They are demo/research only (ADR 001).
- A commercial pilot needs two things:
  1. a damage model retrained on licensed data, such as the dealer's own body-shop photos;
  2. either an Ultralytics Enterprise licence or a non-AGPL stack.

**5. Why not connect to the real police or insurer registry?**
- We have no access, and the hackathon rules forbid real personal or confidential data.
- The registry connector is a sandbox that serves synthetic records. Each record has `"synthetic": true`, the UI shows a SYNTHETIC DEMO DATA badge, and synthetic history is only ever attached to synthetic VINs (CLAUDE.md section 2).
- A real integration is on the roadmap and would need data agreements.

**6. What exactly is synthetic?**
- **Synthetic:** owners, accidents, insurance claims and customer records (generated by `scripts/generate_synthetic.py`, fixed seed).
- **Real:**
  - the video, photos and VIN photo we captured;
  - the VIN decode (WMI table and NHTSA vPIC);
  - the repair prices: public listings with source and date (`data/reference/repair_prices.csv`);
  - the shops (OpenStreetMap).

**7. How do you protect privacy?**
- **Blur first and fail closed.** Plates and faces are blurred before any pixel is shown or saved. If a detector fails, the frame is dropped.
- **Run1 result:** 72 of 72 frames anonymised, 0 dropped (`outputs/e2e/run1/result.json`).
- **Audit.** Three reviewers audited the processed images. One known limitation is stated: an edge-on plate no detector finds (`docs/research/smoke_test_results.md`, Observations 5).
- **Other safeguards:**
  - 3D uses only anonymised frames;
  - VINs are masked in docs;
  - `outputs/` is not committed.

**8. What does an inspection cost?**
- **Marginal cost: $0.** There is no paid API, and HF CPU Basic is $0/hour.
- **Fixed cost:** HF PRO, $9/month (ADR 003).
- **Per inspection:** at the base ASSUMPTION of 240 inspections a month, $0.0375 or 446 UZS (`cost_model.md` section 5).
- Team time and integration are not costed.

**9. How long does an inspection take on CPU?**
- On the free 2-vCPU host it takes minutes.
- **Proxy:** 106.4 s, and 212.7 s with the x2 safety factor ("2-thread pinned proxy, optimistic lower bound", `cost_model.md` section 3).
- **GPU:** the full pipeline took 25.3 s, measured on the dev laptop (RTX 5050 / Ryzen 7 260).
- **Bottleneck:** privacy blurring, more than the damage model.
- **Next steps:**
  - fewer frames;
  - privacy at 1280 px;
  - a GPU option (T4 at $0.40/hour, `cost_model.md` section 2).
- The Space itself is measured in Phase 4.

**10. Why Hugging Face?**
- A Docker Space on CPU Basic gives 2 vCPU and 16 GB RAM, which is enough for our ONNX models, at $0/hour. Creating one requires PRO at $9/month (HF docs change of 2026-07-21).
- Render free has 0.1 CPU and 512 MB, too weak for segmentation. Render paid costs $25/month for 1 CPU (ADR 003).
- The deploy runs from this repo, as the rules require.

**11. Can it scale to many branches?**
- One CPU Basic Space handles one inspection at a time: about 16.9 an hour at the planning figure (`cost_model.md` section 3).
- In the high ASSUMPTION of 3 branches with 20 inspections a day, that is 3.55 compute hours a day (`cost_model.md` section 5), so one Space fits if uploads are spread across the day.
- **Beyond that:** more Spaces, CPU Upgrade ($0.03/hour), or a GPU tier.
- Storage needs a plan in the high scenario. The pilot would exceed 100 GB at the measured 47.8 MB per inspection.

**12. Is there an offline or edge option?**
- Yes. The same app runs on a local machine, the dev laptop in our tests. Phones connect over the branch Wi-Fi, or through a temporary tunnel for testing (ADR 003).
- A branch PC with a GPU would remove hosting cost and the upload wait. It still needs internet for vPIC, the map and the timestamp, or cached fallbacks (the WMI table is local).

**13. How accurate is the 3D model, and what is it for?**
- It is a visual aid, not a measurement tool. Depth Anything 3 gives relative depth and camera poses; it is not metric (`is_metric` is empty in the results files).
- Its job is to show where on the car each damage pin sits, so the customer and the advisor share one picture.
- Measured on 29 anonymised frames:
  - GPU app module: 13.3 s;
  - CPU pinned to 2 threads: 96.6 s (`cost_model.md` section 4).
- It is built after the passport, so it never delays it.

**14. Why trust the repair prices?**
- They are public "from" prices: lower bounds with source URL and date (`data/reference/repair_prices.csv`).
- The UI shows the kind ("from"), the source and the date, never a bare exact-looking number.
- They are not dealer prices, and they vary by car class. The fix is the partner's own price list.

**15. Where does the ROI come from?**
- **Illustrative only.** No partner data was available.
- Every driver is a formula with ASSUMPTION inputs. The value drivers are disputes avoided, repair upsell, accessory upsell and advisor time. The price inputs come from the observed table, and the tables are generated by `scripts/roi_calc.py` (`roi_model.md`).
- **The robust claim:** fewer than two upsold "from"-priced repairs a month pay for hosting. Everything else waits for the partner's numbers.

**16. Does the app save advisor time?**
- Not necessarily on the free CPU host. If the advisor waits several minutes for processing, the low scenario is negative.
- It saves time if processing runs in the background or on a GPU. The paper walkaround time is an ASSUMPTION until timed in the pilot (`roi_model.md`).

**17. Is the PDF tamper-proof?**
- The PDF carries a SHA-256 hash of the source frames and a timestamp, so any change to the frames shows.
- A third-party RFC 3161 timestamp (freetsa) was researched, but it is not in the code yet, and freetsa has no SLA (`cost_model.md` section 5). For legal weight, the dealer would use a trusted timestamp authority.

**18. How does the repeat-inspection diff work?**
- Passports are stored per VIN. A new inspection is compared with the previous one, keyed by part and damage type. The result is new, resolved, worse, improved or unchanged (`src/avtopassport/passport/diff.py`).
- The diff inherits the detector's false positives, so calibration (question 3) matters here too.

**19. What does the jury see live, and what is precomputed?**
- Everything in the demo runs live on the public URL. Nothing is precomputed and presented as live (CLAUDE.md section 2).
- If the 3D model is still building, the page says so.
